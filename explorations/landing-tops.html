Five openers for the top of the page

Five ways the landing page could begin. None of them lead with a call to action — each one instead uses the first screen to tell you something true about SRI, on the theory that a government or a partner arrives wanting to understand the method, not to be converted.

All five are built in the same quiet design language and use SRI-2030's existing words. The sidebar stub is drawn in each so you can judge the composition as a real page top rather than a slide.

The SRI paradox

More rice. Less seed, water and emissions.

  • More Rice — improved yield and better returns for farmers
  • Less Seed — single seedlings, spaced widely, in a grid of 25 cm x 25 cm
  • Less Water — watering not flooding, by alternate wetting and drying
  • Less Energy — no tillage, and fewer bought inputs
  • Lower Emissions — drier soils release far less methane

The System of Rice Intensification is a way of cultivating rice that applies simple principles to achieve remarkable results. The principles of SRI:

  • Reducing competition

    Seeding singly and spacing widely — for example in a grid of 25 cm x 25 cm.

  • Establishing the new crop well

    Transplanting young seedlings, or direct drilling the seed.

  • Watering not flooding

    For example by alternate wetting and drying.

  • Building soil fertility

    Organic manures where feasible; avoiding tillage; cover crops; intercropping with legumes.

Where SRI comes from

SRI was created with farmers, in Madagascar.

It has been carried since by farmers, researchers and extension agents on four continents — and it is still evolving.

  • Madagascar

    Father Henri de Laulanié SJ created SRI with farmers.

  • 1994 · Cornell University

    Professor Norman Uphoff took up SRI and helped to spread it across the globe.

  • Today · Africa

    SRI-2030 works with national governments to scale it — towards 50 million hectares by 2030.
