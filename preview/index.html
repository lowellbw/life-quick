Three ways the site could look

Same content throughout — SRI-2030's own words, logo and colours. What changes is the design language: how much colour it uses, how the page is structured, and how loudly it speaks.

Each version has both pages built. Once you're inside one, the bar at the bottom of the screen switches between all three on whichever page you're looking at, so you can compare like for like.

Version 1

Briefing

Opens like a briefing paper: a masthead rule, the mission stated as the headline, then the numbers behind it. Sections are numbered, and the methodology is a plain list carrying the logo's own four glyphs.

  • Warm off-white, navy type, hairline rules
  • Small splashes of the logo colours on section numbers and glyphs
  • Most credible with governments, funders and partners
Mission People
Version 2

Quiet

The most understated of the three. A low-key fixed sidebar, a narrow column of text, and a great deal of whitespace. Almost monochrome — colour appears only where it has to.

  • Leads with the paradox: “More rice. Less seed, water and emissions.”
  • Colour bands replaced entirely by hairlines and space
  • Reads as considered and editorial; least sales-like
Mission People
Version 3

Bold

The loudest and most branded. Full-bleed lime and navy bands, and the four SRI principles blown up into big saturated tiles drawn straight from the logo's own quadrants.

  • Flat and geometric — no shadows, no rounded cards
  • A four-colour brand strip under the header and above the footer
  • Most immediately recognisable; most energetic
Mission People

What we'd find useful

The three aren't meant to be picked whole — elements move between them. The most useful thing to know is which opening feels right, since that is the real difference: Briefing leads with the mission and the numbers, Quiet leads with the paradox, Bold leads with the brand.

All copy is taken verbatim from the current site, so nothing here is a proposed rewrite — if some of it should change, that's a separate and welcome conversation.
